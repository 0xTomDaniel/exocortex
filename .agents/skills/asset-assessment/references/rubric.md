# Complete Asset-Assessment Rubric

This file defines the conceptual factor set. For repeatable crypto and public-stock evaluation, operationalize it with the stable common, class, subtype, and exposure CSV banks listed in `question-bank-governance.md`; do not regenerate the canonical baseline for each case.

## Canonical output layers and dimensions

Keep these outputs separate as diagnostics. For comparative cases, also retain a predeclared weighted **overall research score** above them; never show the scalar without its components, evidence coverage, gate state, and authorization ceiling:

1. **Merit**
   - `durability` — underlying use, adoption, economics, moat, stewardship, and adaptability.
   - `value_capture` — likelihood underlying success benefits the exact investable instrument.
   - `meme_cultural_capital` — durable cultural distribution and economic conversion.
2. **Entry**
   - `valuation` — expected return, dilution, downside, and embedded expectations at current price.
   - `market_structure` — catalysts, positioning, reflexivity, float, price discovery, and reference-size market liquidity.
3. **Integrity**
   - `security` — instrument-level technical, accounting, operational, dependency, and failure survivability.
   - `control` — governance, unilateral powers, issuance, and accountability.
   - `legal_claim` — identity, title, enforceability, seniority, and lawful holder access.
4. **Implementation**
   - `implementation` — custody, venue, bridge, wrapper-as-route, settlement, and proposed-position exit/slippage.
5. **Fit**
   - `portfolio_fit` — contribution relative to holdings, liabilities, reserves, constraints, taxes, burden, and alternatives.
6. **Evidence** — derived coverage, recency, independence, conflicts, and provenance; never scored as investment merit.

The conceptual factors below map into these layers according to what a question protects. For example, regulation that threatens the business belongs to durability; regulation that strands holder access belongs to legal claim.

## Factors

### 1. Use case and necessity

- Problem solved and beneficiary.
- Durability and frequency of demand.
- Whether the chosen architecture is necessary or merely fashionable.
- Monetary, productive, contractual, consumptive, status, cultural, or hybrid utility.

### 2. Adoption and product-market fit

- Active users, customers, retention, cohorts, and concentration.
- Transactions, trading, borrowing, settlement, occupancy, sales, or other category-appropriate activity.
- Organic versus subsidized use.
- Quality and stickiness of integrations and distribution.
- For crypto, TVL quality and composition—not TVL alone.

### 3. Economics and quality of activity

- Revenue, gross fees, net revenue, margins, free cash flow, and holder distributions as distinct concepts.
- Recurring versus cyclical, subsidized, manipulable, or related-party activity.
- Unit economics, operating leverage, capital intensity, and cost structure.
- Who pays, why, and whether willingness to pay persists.

Never equate TVL with revenue, gross fees with profit, protocol revenue with token-holder earnings, or trading volume with organic demand.

### 4. Instrument value capture

- Legal/economic claim on cash flow, assets, redemption, governance, security, utility, or scarcity.
- Dividends, buybacks, burns, fee conversion, distributions, rent, staking, collateral demand, or monetary premium.
- Whether value capture is automatic, discretionary, revocable, indirect, or nonexistent.
- Whether the underlying company/protocol/network can prosper while the investable instrument does not.
- Seniority and dilution relative to other claimants.

### 5. Supply, dilution, and ownership distribution

- Current float/circulating supply, total/max supply, and fully diluted value.
- Issuance, emissions, equity compensation, vesting, unlocks, refinancing, and treasury sales.
- Founder, insider, foundation, government, whale, creditor, and market-maker concentration.
- Cost basis and incentives of marginal sellers.
- Float dynamics, short interest, and hidden or contingent claims.

### 6. Valuation and embedded expectations

- Price relative to category-appropriate cash flow, revenue, assets, replacement cost, adoption, monetary base, scarcity, or credible comparables.
- Circulating market value versus fully diluted or enterprise value.
- Bull/base/bear scenarios with explicit assumptions.
- What growth, margins, adoption, regulation, and terminal status are already priced in.
- Expected value and downside relative to the next-best use of capital.

### 7. Memetic strength and cultural capital

Treat memetic strength as a first-class factor across crypto, public and private stocks, commodities, currencies, real estate, funds, art, collectibles, brands, and luxury assets.

Assess:
- **Legibility:** can the reason to care be transmitted in one memorable sentence, symbol, person, or image?
- **Symbolic density:** how much cultural meaning is compressed into the asset?
- **Identity formation:** do holders, customers, employees, or residents identify with it and advocate voluntarily?
- **Organic propagation:** is culture created bottom-up or purchased/coordinated by insiders?
- **Distribution power:** reach, recruitment rate, share of mind, and geographic/language portability.
- **Durability:** survival through price declines, boredom, ridicule, scandal, competition, and leadership changes.
- **Narrative elasticity:** capacity to adapt while retaining a coherent identity.
- **Economic conversion:** translation into holders, customers, talent, liquidity, pricing power, fees, integrations, financing access, lower cost of capital, collateral acceptance, political protection, or monetary premium.
- **Authenticity and concentration:** dependence on one founder, influencer, platform, or promotional budget.
- **Reflexive fragility:** whether rising price is the meme's only sustaining evidence.

Distinguish:
1. durable cultural capital;
2. fundamentals amplified by a strong meme;
3. reflexive attention temporarily outrunning fundamentals;
4. manufactured promotion without durable culture.

Meme can create or reshape fundamentals, but it is not automatically durable or sufficient.

### 8. Competitive moat

- Network, liquidity, data, distribution, scale, regulatory, brand, cultural, cost, and switching-cost advantages.
- Developer, supplier, customer, and complement ecosystems.
- Forkability, replicability, substitute quality, and barriers to entry.
- Dependence on incentives or exclusive relationships.
- Ability to retain advantage as technology and regulation change.

### 9. Security, technical, operational, and custody risk

- Exploit, fraud, outage, default, oracle, bridge, consensus, title, counterparty, and operational history as applicable.
- Audits, controls, insurance, upgradeability, admin keys, emergency/freeze powers, and dependency surface.
- Custody, recovery, settlement, title, rehypothecation, and jurisdiction.
- Response under stress and treatment of affected holders/users.
- Case-specific lived evidence; do not dismiss concrete failures because aggregate metrics look strong.

### 10. Decentralization, governance, and control

- Validator, sequencer, board, voting, ownership, oracle, management, trustee, sponsor, or government concentration.
- Multisig and unilateral control powers.
- Governance participation, capture resistance, minority-holder rights, and credible neutrality.
- Censorship, confiscation, amendment, and succession risk.

### 11. Team, management, and capital allocation

- Execution and shipping record.
- Honesty, transparency, competence, adaptability, and incentive alignment.
- Capital allocation, treasury policy, acquisitions, issuance, and related-party behavior.
- Key-person, succession, culture, and governance risk.
- Conduct during failures rather than only during growth.

### 12. Regulatory, legal, tax, and geopolitical exposure

- Asset classification, licensing, sanctions, securities, commodities, lending, derivatives, payments, privacy, zoning, title, and consumer-protection exposure.
- Enforcement surface, jurisdiction, political constituency, and regulatory adaptability.
- Tax treatment, reporting burden, and potential structural changes.
- Whether regulation affects a peripheral activity or defeats the core thesis.

### 13. Liquidity and market structure

- Genuine depth, slippage, spread, turnover, and exit capacity under stress.
- Venue, dealer, custodian, platform, and geographic concentration.
- Wash trading, manipulation, short/borrow availability, leverage, funding, and liquidation structure.
- Settlement time and transaction costs.
- Difference between quoted value and realizable value.

### 14. Catalysts and path dependence

- Product releases, distributions, buybacks, fee changes, integrations, approvals, refinancing, unlocks, litigation, regulation, and macro shifts.
- Probability, timing, dependency chain, and whether the catalyst is already priced.
- Ability to survive until the thesis resolves.
- Distinguish durable thesis evidence from a temporary narrative catalyst.

### 15. Price action, positioning, and reflexivity

- Distance from highs/lows and recent acceleration.
- Crowdedness, holder profitability, cost basis, flows, open interest, funding, short interest, and leverage.
- Forced buyers/sellers and reflexive feedback loops.
- Use for entry and risk control, not as standalone proof of value.

### 16. Failure modes and falsifiability

- Strongest steelmanned bear case.
- Permanent-impairment routes versus recoverable volatility.
- Expected drawdown and liquidity under the adverse case.
- Explicit thesis invalidators, monitoring evidence, stop/review conditions, and reopen conditions.
- Correlated failures and second-order consequences.

### 17. Portfolio fit and opportunity cost

- Intended role: core, income, growth, tactical, hedge, venture, cultural/collectible, or consumption hybrid.
- Correlation and factor overlap with existing assets and liabilities.
- Concentration, maximum tolerable loss, time horizon, cash-flow needs, and psychological capacity.
- Custody, operational, tax, legal, and attention burden.
- Protected cash/runway and funds that must not be risked.
- Better alternatives: keep/expand an existing holding, diversify, hold cash, reduce debt, or invest in the investor's own productive capacity.

### 18. Evidence quality and uncertainty

- Primary documents, audited records, on-chain data, legal title, direct observation, reputable independent research, dashboards, marketing, social claims, and inference.
- Data recency, methodology, coverage, revisions, and conflicts.
- Missing evidence and unresolved contradictions.
- Selection, survivorship, promotional, and confirmation bias.
- Never assign precision or familiarity unsupported by checked evidence.

## Category adjustments

Weight, do not omit, factors based on the asset:

- **Monetary assets:** security, scarcity/issuance credibility, liquidity, decentralization/institutional trust, adoption, and memetic/monetary premium dominate; revenue multiples may be inapplicable.
- **Operating companies/stocks:** durable cash generation, reinvestment, governance, dilution, competitive moat, capital allocation, valuation, and memetic effects on customers/talent/capital dominate.
- **Protocol tokens:** product-market fit, security, instrument value capture, supply/unlocks, control, regulation, and valuation dominate.
- **L1/L2 networks:** security budget, usage, fees, developer/ecosystem retention, state/liquidity concentration, interoperability, and dilution dominate.
- **Privacy assets:** transaction utility, privacy set/technology, censorship resistance, liquidity/access, and regulation dominate.
- **Real estate:** title, location, replacement supply, cash yield, financing, maintenance, regulation/tax, liquidity, and place narrative dominate.
- **Collectibles/art/luxury:** authenticity, provenance, canonical status, condition, scarcity, cultural durability, holder network, transaction costs, and liquidity dominate.
- **Private/venture assets:** team, market, distribution, financing path, governance rights, dilution, runway, optionality, and exit routes dominate.
- **Microcaps/memecoins:** ownership/control, liquidity, contract/security, promotion authenticity, supply, market structure, memetic durability, and exit capacity dominate.

## Required contradiction check

Before a final or highest-conviction conclusion, verify:

- No known security, legal, governance, ownership, liquidity, or trust disqualifier was ignored.
- Underlying success plausibly reaches the instrument.
- Current valuation has not silently replaced asset quality in the reasoning—or vice versa.
- The meme is not sustained only by price appreciation or paid promotion.
- Supply/unlocks and realizable liquidity are understood.
- User-specific protected capital and concentration constraints are known before recommending action.
- Confidence matches evidence quality.
